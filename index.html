FROM DIAGNOSIS TO DESIGN

Four links. Four different questions.

More accuracy cannot repair
every kind of assurance failure.

01 / COMPARISON

Better by which standard?

When evaluations change, establish continuity in the respect claimed—or explain why the change is justified.

02 / COVERAGE

What never reached the test?

Recognition, jurisdiction, and the monitored boundary determine what a checker can see.

03 / EFFECT

What does “no” actually stop?

A correct objection needs the right timing and authority if it is meant to prevent an action.

04 / EVIDENCE

Would failure look different?

Clean records discriminate only if relevant failures would probably leave a different record.

These links diagnose arrangements meant both to inform and to govern action. A claim about a property alone needs only the links relevant to that claim; an informative test does not always need enforcement authority. Chapter 2, §2.6

NO ASSURANCE FOR FREE

The answer isn’t
to stop checking.
It’s to name the grounds.

The book argues that sustained assurance beyond an evaluator’s range carries a price: narrower claims, restricted use, slower development, or independently supported grounds.

Those prices can be modest and worth paying. The important question is whether the grounds remain adequate as the system, its evidence, and its standards change.

THE SOURCE

No Assurance for Free

Evaluation, Trust, and the Limits of Self-Improving AI

Blake A. Myers

This interactive companion translates arguments from the manuscript into small, inspectable experiments. Chapter and section references accompany each experiment. The models are illustrations, not empirical forecasts or proofs of general AI unsafety.

Method, scope, and limitations

Based on the supplied manuscript, no-assurance-for-free(7).docx. Text here is an explanatory adaptation. Numerical examples and controls were constructed for this site unless explicitly identified as the book’s example.

The book’s arguments are conditional. They do not establish that every evaluator fails, every delegation is unjustified, every stronger system is untrustworthy, or every AI influence amounts to capture. Boundary restrictions, checkable results, and independent evidence can support bounded reliance. No simulation here certifies a real system.

The full manuscript is not hosted here. No analytics, remote fonts, or third-party scripts are used. Notes in the assurance worksheet stay on this device only if you choose to save them.
